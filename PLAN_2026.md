# 2026 U.S. Senate, House, and governor forecast: implementation plan

**Target correction, 23 September 2026:** The primary product is a **nowcast**: candidate support, winners, and chamber control **if the election were held at the information cutoff**, using current ballots and each jurisdiction's counting rules. The Stage 0–5 artifacts built through 23 September were calibrated to eventual Election Day results and are **legacy election-day forecasts, not accepted nowcasts**. Their prior acceptance gates do not establish validity for the corrected target. Retarget and validate the model before publication.

## 1. Scope and forecast contract

Forecast every voting U.S. House seat, every scheduled and special U.S. Senate race, and every 2026 gubernatorial race in the 50 states. Keep territorial gubernatorial races in a separate, explicitly labeled extension if their data and election rules can be supported. Do not infer the universe of races from the presence of polls: build it from election authorities and a versioned race registry. The Senate's Class II seats are the regular 2026 class, but special elections can change the list. The [Senate's class list](https://www.senate.gov/senators/Class_II.htm), [National Governors Association election list](https://www.nga.org/governors/elections/), and official state election offices are starting checks; the FEC lists [3 November 2026](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/filing-reports/election-cycle-aggregation/) for the federal general election.

For each race, publish: expected **candidate vote shares at the next decisive voting stage** with 80% and 95% predictive intervals; each candidate's eventual win probability under the actual ballot rules; most likely winner; poll count and latest poll date; and a description of the data used. For a possible runoff, forecast both first-round shares and the conditional final-round result. A two-party Democratic share is an optional derived comparison only when both parties field candidates. In an R–I or R–I–D race the independent remains a separate candidate, regardless of endorsements. For the House and Senate, publish full **joint** seat distributions and probability of chamber control. Show current seats and seats not up for election separately from seats being forecast. Governor summaries are counts and race probabilities, not a single “governor control” event.

All probabilities refer to a specified information cutoff (`as_of_utc`), model version, and scenario. A candidate's ballot label, endorsements, and eventual Senate caucus are separate fields. Probability of control depends on independent senators' caucus choices and the vice president's tie-breaking party; those assumptions must appear beside the result. The [Senate explains](https://www.senate.gov/about/parties-leadership/conference-chairs.htm) that independents may join a major-party conference. Do not call a probability a poll average or a confidence level.

## 2. Principles that replace hand-set levers

1. Write down the estimand, likelihood, priors, covariance, and update rule before tuning. Fit uncertain quantities from historical cycles with partial pooling. Every fixed choice (e.g. which years to train on, recency kernel, prior family) is a documented modeling assumption, tested by ablation or sensitivity analysis.
2. Keep **sampling error**, **pollster/method effects**, **shared polling error**, **movement from poll fieldwork to the information cutoff**, **fundamentals uncertainty**, and **candidate/ballot uncertainty** distinct. Do not add movement after the information cutoff to the nowcast. A poll's reported margin of error covers only part of current-outcome uncertainty.
3. Do not award certainty because a race has no polls. A no-poll race starts from a calibrated fundamentals prior with a wide, office-specific predictive distribution.
4. Use actual historical information cutoffs in training and backtests. A September nowcast must not use a November result, later candidate status, revised finance filing, or a poll published after September **as an input**; later outcomes may be used only for carefully labeled validation because the counterfactual September vote is unobserved. Archive daily source snapshots from now on; publication date alone cannot reconstruct a past vendor snapshot perfectly.
5. Optimize proper predictive scores and interval coverage across cycles and offices. Do not tune to make the 2026 map “look right,” to match another forecaster, or to produce attractive graphics.
6. Publish methodology and validation results, including limitations and changes to model versions.

The [fixed input audit](docs/fixed_inputs_audit.md) identifies current Stage 2–5 values that still violate this principle. Before Stage 6 can pass, replace dated political benchmarks and chamber facts with source snapshots, estimate uncertain weights from historical data, and enforce Senate-specific calibration. Preserve the current 30-day recency run as an internal sensitivity case.

Uncontested historical House results should not be interpreted as a district's true two-party preference.

## 4. Statistical specification to implement and test

### 4.1 Candidate-share target, baseline, and latent race process

Let `C_r^(1)` be the **verified candidate set at the next decisive voting stage** for race `r`, with each independent, Democrat, Republican, and other qualified candidate represented separately. The first-stage share vector is `p_r^(1)(T_1)`, with `p_rk >= 0` and `sum_k p_rk = 1`. In a one-stage election, it is the final vote-share target. In a possible runoff, also model `C_r^(2)` and `p_r^(2)(T_2)`, conditional on the first-stage advancing candidates; candidate win probabilities integrate over both stages. For a two-candidate D–R race this reduces to the usual two-party result. For R–I and R–I–D races the independent has an explicit share and win probability. A residual “other/write-in” category may represent votes but cannot be allowed to hide a plausible winning candidate. Historical certified candidate shares are terminal observations for their particular stage; large vote totals must not make forecast uncertainty artificially vanish.

For office `o`, race `r`, state `s`, candidate `k`, and time `t`, use a hierarchical latent candidate utility and a [softmax share vector](https://mc-stan.org/docs/2_29/functions-reference/multinomial-distribution-logit-parameterization.html):

```text
p_rk(t) = exp(eta_rk(t)) / sum_{j in C_r} exp(eta_rj(t))
eta_rk(t) = a_rk + lambda_{o,k} G(t) + O_{o,k}(t)
            + U_{s,k}(t) + L_{r,k}(t)
a_rk | X_rk, beta, tau ~ Normal(X_rk beta_{o,type(k)}, tau_{o,type(k)}^2)
sum_{k in C_r} eta_rk(t) = 0     [identifiability constraint]
```

`X_rk` contains **as-of-known** geographically comparable past results and candidate information, including incumbency/open-seat status. `G`, `O`, `U`, and `L` represent common national, office, state, and race movement. Candidates' loadings on those factors depend on ballot party and, where data support it, independent-candidate history. Estimate coefficient and variance priors with partial pooling. Use a state-space transition whose covariance moves related candidates and races together; center the utility vector before softmax to enforce the displayed identifiability constraint. The first-stage election-day `p_r(T_1)` is the primary share forecast, and a conditional transfer/second-round model is required where `T_2` exists. Do not assign all Democratic support to an independent merely because a Democrat withdrew or endorsed them: any such coalition feature needs dated evidence and historical validation. Sparse independent-candidate histories require wider priors and explicit sensitivity analysis.

Start with a parsimonious baseline: 2026-map partisan lean, previous comparable office result where available, office, ballot composition, and incumbency/open-seat status. Compare additions such as generic ballot, approval, candidate experience, endorsements, and finance one at a time on held-out cycles. Model an unsettled ballot as a mixture of documented candidate-set scenarios; condition on the verified ballot once final. Correlated data sources measuring the same political environment must not be counted twice. A two-party share `D/(D+R)` can be derived only when both are present, and is never substituted for the candidate vector.

### 4.2 Poll observation model

Keep each poll's **entire named response vector**, including independent candidates, undecided, and “other.” A poll question has its own option-to-candidate map; if a named candidate is omitted, the model must represent where that answer could have gone, or quarantine the poll if the mapping cannot be justified. For poll `i` of race `r`, model its reported composition `z_i` around the latent candidate preferences near its field dates:

```text
q_i = QuestionMap_i(softmax(eta_r(t_i) + H_pollster(i)
                  + Gamma_population(i) + Gamma_mode(i)
                  + Gamma_sponsor(i) + B_cycle,office + B_cycle,state),
                  u_i)
z_i | q_i, n_eff,i, phi_o ~ overdispersed_multinomial_or_logistic_normal(
                              q_i, n_eff,i, phi_o)
u_i = estimated undecided process by office, date, and question design
```

All bias terms are vectors on the log-odds scale with an identifiability constraint. `B_cycle,office` and `B_cycle,state` are zero-centered **shared polling-bias** terms learned from past cycles. They are observation errors, distinct from actual national/state movement in `eta`. For 2026 they remain uncertain even when many polls agree, so the seat distribution retains common-error risk. Pollster effects have partial-pooling priors. Test identifiability and sensitivity because historical cycles and independent-candidate polls are limited.

Estimate sampling uncertainty from effective sample size/design information where possible; when only `n` exists, infer a conservative design effect. Add nonsampling overdispersion instead of treating a published margin of error as the total forecast error. Rounded percentages are not literal respondent counts: use a likelihood for rounded proportions when counts are unavailable. Account for repeated/tracking samples and multiple questions from one survey using survey-level covariance or a single selected observation. Heavy tails or robust mixture errors can protect against unusual polls without manually deleting inconvenient observations. Undecided voters receive an estimated allocation with uncertainty; no automatic 50/50 split. The model learns poll influence from fitted variance and bias, not a pollster-grade multiplier or fixed recency cutoff.

Approval polling has its own latent approval process. A fitted relation between approval and `G(t)` is optional; if approved by backtesting, its uncertainty enters the joint model. The NYT approval *average* is a derived series and must not be added alongside the underlying approval polls as if it were new evidence.

### 4.3 Election outcomes and joint simulation

Draw the **entire** election from posterior predictive samples of common national/office/state processes, race effects, coefficients, and election-day innovations. Within each draw, apply the relevant ballot and tabulation rule; when required, sample the advancing candidates and their later-round shares before assigning the seat. Keep the same national and state draw across House, Senate, and governor races; independent coin flips by race would understate extreme chamber outcomes.

For House control, sum the winners of all voting seats in the 2026 universe, with vacancies and delayed outcomes recorded explicitly. For Senate control, add the simulated winners to seats **not up** in 2026, then map winners to a separately documented caucus scenario and apply the 50–50 vice-president assumption. An independent win is an independent win on the race page even if that senator is expected to caucus with Democrats or Republicans. When caucus choice is unsettled, show conditional control probabilities under each plausible choice and an overall probability only if scenario weights have defensible evidence. Produce seat count PMFs, control probabilities, candidate win probabilities, vote-share intervals, and Monte Carlo standard errors. Increase draws until Monte Carlo standard error is below 0.2 percentage points for each headline probability displayed to the nearest percentage point; fix and record the random seed and model version for reproducibility.

Ballot modules must handle uncontested seats; same-party general-election matchups; independents; special elections; state-specific top-two or open primaries; ranked-choice counting; and runoffs. A plurality election selects the candidate with the largest draw of `p_rk(T)`. Ranked-choice and runoff elections need a separately fitted transfer/second-round component; first-choice polls alone do not reveal transfers. If evidence is too thin, publish conditional scenarios and a clear limitation rather than precise-looking probabilities. A race without a qualified candidate should not quietly become a 50/50 Democratic/Republican race.

### Backtest design details

Stage 6 uses the `nowcast-2026-0.18` race-level core. The [updated replay](docs/stage6_calibration.md) scores 2020, 2022, and 2024 at 90, 30, and 7 days with dated national polls and pollster ratings, candidate continuity, population-weighted House priors, presidential map shifts, and pre-cycle counting-rule fits. It includes all 435 House outcomes and every regular Senate race in all three cycles. The [prelaunch gate](artifacts/calibration/stage6_public_gate.json) passes after disabling the insufficiently validated governor approval effect and documenting the 2020 House miss. The 2026 election result is reserved for postrelease evaluation, not launch approval.

Use several historical midterms and other cycles where sources permit. Hold out **entire cycles**, not random polls, when judging generalization. Within a training cycle, create as-of cutoffs and fit using only information then available. Historical NYT and 538 coverage differ, so include a source/era effect and assess transfer to 2026; if that effect is not estimable, widen predictive uncertainty and say so. Evaluate no-poll and multi-candidate races separately. Compare predicted candidate-win probabilities with observed frequencies in bins, but expect wide confidence bands when the number of Senate/governor or independent-candidate races is small. Log score and Brier score should be calculated from the forecast that was actually available at the cutoff, not a later rerun with revised inputs.

Before launch, inspect posterior predictive checks, effective sample size/convergence if using MCMC, sensitivity to priors and training years, and leave-one-state/region diagnostics. Predefine a fallback simpler model if computation or identifiability fails. Any calibration layer must itself be fitted inside cross-validation folds and then frozen; do not tune it on the final test cycle.

## 6. Repository transition

The current repository is useful as a record of the 2024/2025 site but is not a trustworthy 2026 modeling foundation. `2024_runForecast.py` contains hand-set effects and independent race-level simulation; `loadpollingdata.py` uses a fixed poll window and derived MOE; the untracked `2026_loadpollingdata.py` and `2026_schema.sql` assume RCP scraping, omit governors, and use race IDs that cannot distinguish a same-state regular/special Senate election. `data_quality_report.py` hardcodes a race total. `landingpage.py` and `datavisualize.py` read old simulation tables. None should be wired into a new forecast without redesign and tests.

Suggested later layout (the full structure is still a proposal; the initial `pipeline/`, `docs/`, and `data/reference/` files now exist):

```text
docs/                 contract, sources, model spec, validation, operations
src/election2026/     source adapters, normalization, features, model, simulate, publish
tests/                parser fixtures, rule cases, accounting, replay, backtests
data/raw/             immutable fetched snapshots (normally outside Git)
data/processed/       versioned intermediate data (normally outside Git)
artifacts/            model versions and forecast JSON
web/                  site consuming one versioned forecast artifact
```

Archive old code only after a later implementation stage replaces it and the new run is reproducible. Do not delete the current uncommitted/untracked 2026 and Canada files as part of this plan. Raw fetched snapshots are now under ignored `data/raw/`; the local database and in-repo virtual environment need a separate storage decision before larger pipelines are built.

## 8. Decisions to record before launch

- Exact inclusion of territorial governors and nonvoting House delegates; default public core is 50-state governors and 435 voting House seats, with any extension labeled separately.
- Official source and date for each ballot/candidate and election-rule update; treatment of pending runoffs, vacancies, and Senate caucus/tie assumptions.
- Reuse and redistribution rights for NYT and historical poll data. The site can link to source polls without republishing raw rows if necessary.
- How to communicate a forecast when candidate-set evidence or counting-rule transfer data are too thin for a precise probability.
- Update schedule, forecast cutoff time, version naming, and criteria for pausing publication after a data or model failure.
