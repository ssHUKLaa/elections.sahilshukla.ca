# Structural ballot uncertainty in the 2026 nowcast

The nowcast fits a distribution of candidate shares before applying polls. A previous election with two major-party candidates gives weak guidance when an independent joins the ballot, and especially when one major party is absent. The 2026 prior now distinguishes these structures using historical transitions from the Stage 2 database.

For a new other-party candidate alongside both major parties, historical transition residuals are centered by cycle and office, then by state and cycle. Within each office, residuals are standardized by their local standard deviation. The ratio of the pooled new-other standard deviation to the ordinary-ballot standard deviation multiplies **only the race-specific** prior error in each log-ratio coordinate. Ratios below one are held at one because this adjustment is intended to represent added structural uncertainty. The fitted ratios and sample counts are in `artifacts/nowcast/model_parameters.json` under `structural_prior.other_debut`. Multiple independents do not trigger an additional multiplier: the historical evidence did not support it.

If the ballot has an independent and only one major party, the ordinary D/R transition is poorly identified. We fit the log of major-party to other-party vote from 103 historical races with the same broad structure. Candidate predictors are the prior major-party lean and office indicators; the ridge penalty is chosen by leaving out each election cycle in turn, with a fully pooled intercept-only model also eligible. The fitted residual standard deviation supplies the prior uncertainty. Senate analogs are scarce (two races), so the fit can select a pooled center. The distribution still has substantial uncertainty, and a race with no polls remains sensitive to this model choice.

The polling update remains Gaussian. If its effective prior candidate log-ratio covariance is `P`, the poll observation matrix is `H`, and poll-error covariance is `R`, the gain is `P Hᵀ (H P Hᵀ + R)⁻¹`. Increasing the relevant prior variance increases the influence of the corresponding poll contrast. The published `poll_diagnostics.poll_contrast_leverage` is the diagonal of `H P Hᵀ (H P Hᵀ + R)⁻¹` for each race. These values describe the Gaussian update's local influence; they are not a share of votes or a claim that a poll is unbiased. For a candidate omitted from polls, the existing model preserves its prior mass.

For the two unusual ballot classes, the nowcast also checks whether the candidate-level prior conflicts with the available polls. It estimates a scalar `s ≥ 1` by maximizing the Gaussian marginal likelihood of the observed poll contrasts, with predictive covariance `s H P Hᵀ + R`. The chosen scale widens the prior covariance before the candidate-level update; ordinary ballots retain `s = 1`. This is a race-general empirical-Bayes fit, and `poll_diagnostics.prior_conflict_fit` records the scale, likelihood improvement, and whether the numerical search limit was reached. The same fitted scale is used across the paired fundamentals-impact steps. Because the scale is estimated from the race's own polls, posterior intervals condition on that fitted scale and do not include uncertainty about it.

This rule uses historical ballot structure, not a Montana-specific factor. The model remains a nowcast at the data cutoff. It is not a projection to Election Day.
