# Fundamentals research and implementation agenda (23 September 2026)

## Scope and current baseline

This is a research plan for the **held-today** 2026 House, Senate, and governor nowcast. It does not change forecast parameters. The question is how to estimate present support in an unpolled race; predicting movement between today and Election Day is a separate task.

The model fits national Democratic two-party House vote to 39 elections (1948–2024) using the previous House result, presidential party, midterm status, and presidential approval measured roughly 43 days before Election Day. **Version 0.5 now uses this estimate as a diagnostic and centers the live national environment on the generic ballot**; see [the generic-first update](generic_first_nowcast_2026.md). Race priors largely carry forward the prior seat result, with a separate fitted local-lean equation for 28 regular, two-party Senate races. Candidate allocation primarily knows whether a candidate was the prior winner. Governor priors do not use governor approval. House priors do not systematically use presidential vote tabulated on the **2026 district boundaries**. See [the national rebuild](national_environment_rebuild.md), [the Senate lean rebuild](senate_local_lean_rebuild.md), and `pipeline/build_stage2.py` / `modeling/stage3_results_baseline.py` for implementation details.

The historical approval model's four later held-out elections have 2.95-point RMSE, while the same specification without approval has 2.28-point RMSE. Four cases are inconclusive, but this gives no demonstrated recent-cycle benefit for its current approval coefficient. Also, both models predict an *eventual election result* using earlier data. Their error standard deviations cannot automatically be interpreted as uncertainty about a held-today vote. This target mismatch is the first item to fix.

## Proposed statistical design

1. Define latent national Democratic two-party *current* House preference, `N_t`, separately from eventual House vote `Y_e`. Model a current generic ballot observation as `G_t = N_t + b_G(method, electorate, date) + error`. Approval, economy, and previous elections inform a prior for `N_t`, while an explicit transition `Y_e = N_t + drift(t,e) + election_error` is used **only** in historical outcome validation. Because historical `N_t` is latent, identify it with multiple independent contemporaneous measures where possible; do not claim an election-result backtest directly validates same-day support.
2. Fit approval and generic jointly, for example as correlated measurements of a common national component plus an approval-specific component. A simpler first test is a regularized regression of historical generic-ballot averages on approval and earlier House vote, always holding out entire election cycles. Compare generic-only, generic-plus-approval, and generic-plus-approval-plus-economy. Coefficients, measurement errors, and covariance must be learned from training cycles, never chosen to make 2026 agree with another forecast.
3. For race `r`, transform the national two-party preference to log odds and use `log(D_r/R_r)_t = alpha_office + beta_office * logit(N_t) + L_r + C_r + S_state,t + error_r,t`. `L_r` is local partisan lean from vote on current geography; `C_r` is a candidate/incumbency adjustment; `S_state,t` permits a state mood or governor-popularity signal. Learn office slopes, common shocks, and race residual covariance without treating hundreds of races in one cycle as independent tests of a national effect.
4. Retain a candidate-share simplex for independents and third parties. Fit local partisan lean and candidate allocation separately; an R-vs-I or R-vs-I-vs-D race cannot be forced into a D/R regression. For any independent candidate, record likely caucus as a separate seat-control assumption, not a vote-share feature.

## Validation and release gates

- Reconstruct **as-of** snapshots at several dates per cycle. Block by election cycle, and for governor candidate effects also inspect blocked state/candidate checks. Preserve source publication dates and revisions; never use final candidate lists or maps that were unknown at a simulated historical cutoff.
- Report national margin MAE/RMSE and interval coverage; local vote-share MAE, winner log score, calibration, and coverage by office, incumbent/open seat, contested/uncontested baseline, redistricted geography, and multi-candidate race. Compare each proposal to the current model and to a simple current-generic / presidential-lean benchmark.
- Test the incremental value of each feature by ablation on identical holdout cycles. Given the small number of national elections, show per-cycle errors and uncertainty in the improvement; a one-cycle gain is insufficient evidence.
- Freeze one September 2026 snapshot before changing model parameters. Report exact House/Senate/governor shifts and source lineage after every accepted replacement. Keep research variants out of the public feed until the data and calibration gates pass.

## Concrete work order

1. **Now:** obtain and inspect the Algara replication data, confirm actual poll dates/party coding/availability, and reproduce the paper's congressional generic-versus-approval comparison. Then perform an as-of, cycle-blocked ablation for our national model. This is the quickest test of the current fundamentals risk.
2. **Next:** inventory 2026 House map changes and build district-presidential vote on exact 2026 boundaries; construct historical comparable boundary data. Fit the local House mean and open-seat/candidate effects using cycle holdouts. Prioritize seats where 2024 House vote is uncontested or geography changed.
3. **In parallel after source audit:** acquire dated historical and 2026 governor approval, reproduce the gubernatorial benchmark, then fit an office-specific governor prior. If contemporary coverage is sparse, use wide intervals and the baseline for missing states.
4. **Later:** test macroeconomic and issue variables conditional on generic ballot and approval. Keep only variables with stable incremental gain and a reproducible, dated 2026 feed.

The immediately defensible change is **methodological**: treat the approval prior and generic ballot as correlated, and stop interpreting an Election Day-trained residual as a held-today uncertainty without a temporal model. The coefficient/weight change should follow the first ablation, because existing evidence does not tell us its correct numerical value for this nowcast.

**Preliminary test completed:** [national signal ablation](national_signal_ablation_2026.md). In six rolling 2006–2016 cycles, approval improved prediction of the contemporaneous generic reading but did not consistently improve eventual House-vote prediction once the generic reading was known. The reconstruction and small sample prevent using it as a final weight calibration; the planned strictly dated joint fit remains open.

**Poll-level follow-up completed:** [joint national signal test](joint_national_signal_test_2026.md). FiveThirtyEight's ratings archive expands the overlap to 13 cycles and eight rolling tests, with a similar qualitative result. Its median field dates omit reliable first-publication dates; a 2020 timestamp audit found post-cutoff database entries among polls included by the field-date rule. Thus the release-grade as-of covariance calibration remains open even though the broader provisional test is reproducible.

**Live decision completed:** [version 0.5](generic_first_nowcast_2026.md) used the well-polled generic ballot alone for the shared national center. The owner then requested a [small 5% approval contribution](small_approval_blend_2026.md), implemented for the next version 0.6 run without rerunning the pipeline yet. The previous independent combination remains a sensitivity, and the current-support uncertainty gate remains open.
