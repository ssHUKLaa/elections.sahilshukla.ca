# Senate local-lean rebuild and race audit

**Run date: 22 September 2026. Forecast status: internal.**

The previous Senate baseline carried forward a result from the same seat and moved it with the national House environment. That result mixes state partisanship, the old national environment, and candidate-specific support. The replacement fits those pieces from data. It uses [FiveThirtyEight's aggregated election results](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/election-results) for regular Senate contests and state presidential votes, and [Brookings' House popular vote series](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/vital-statistics-on-congress/) to express each Senate margin relative to its election year's national vote. Sources and hashes are frozen in `data/reference/local_lean/`.

Raw source files are kept out of Git. On a new host, `python modeling/national_environment.py --restore` and `python modeling/download_local_history.py --restore` retrieve the files in the tracked manifests and reject a hash mismatch. Source attribution and redistribution limits are recorded in [data_rights.md](data_rights.md).

For each historical regular Senate race with both major parties, the inputs are the previous result in **that seat class** relative to the previous national House vote; the state's most recent presidential vote relative to the national presidential vote; and whether the Democratic or Republican candidate ran in the previous same-seat election. Presidential votes from the target election are never used as predictors. Fusion ballot lines are assigned to a major-party nominee only when the candidate also has that major-party line. The 2024 presidential state totals were compared to the [FEC's official compilation](https://www.fec.gov/introduction-campaign-finance/election-results-and-voting-information/): all 51 jurisdictions agree within 27 major-party votes.

Ridge candidates were selected by rolling election-cycle predictions through 2018. The 2020, 2022, and 2024 cycles were left for a later check. Among 361 usable historical races, the combined model selected a 0.1 ridge penalty. Its rolling tuning RMSE was **14.8 points of Senate lean**, and its later-cycle RMSE was **9.8 points**. The previous-seat-only alternative had **17.0** and **11.8** points respectively; presidential lean alone had **18.2** and **11.9**. Thus the combined baseline improves held-out race means, though Senate outcomes remain intrinsically noisy. Fitted coefficients are approximately 0.406 on previous Senate lean, 0.459 on presidential lean, and 7.4 points for a returning Democratic candidate (the negative of that for a returning Republican candidate). These are regression estimates, not hand-set levers.

The model yields local predictions for 31 regular 2026 races; 28 have both D and R candidates and receive the D/R mean update. Arkansas and Louisiana lack a suitable two-party prior, and the Florida and Ohio special elections retain their existing baselines. Each updated race keeps its existing correlated uncertainty draws. No Senate-specific interval multiplier was introduced. The national environment and race polls still update the same draws.

In its original isolation run, the local-lean fit moved **Democratic Senate control from 41.9% to 39.5%** under the scenario where all independent winners caucus with Democrats. House control stayed 81.8%. This was a model correction selected on held-out historical races, not a change designed to reach another model's probability. Several local corrections favored Democrats, including Georgia and Maine; others favored Republicans, especially Mississippi. The current [component audit](senate_component_audit.md) isolates local lean within the later recalibrated polling model.

## Remaining polling discrepancy

The [current race audit](../artifacts/senate_race_audit.json) compares Stage 3 priors, a simple 90-day raw poll average with a 30-day half life, and the final model. North Carolina's raw D/R poll margin is about D+10.0 and the current model margin D+4.9; Texas is raw D+2.9 versus model R+1.2; Maine is raw D+2.1 versus model R+1.6. These raw averages do not adjust for pollster bias, field population, shared errors, or candidate omission. In multi-candidate races such as South Carolina, the D/R group margin does not represent one Democrat's chance of winning; inspect the candidate vector instead.

The local-lean result above predates the [multi-cycle Senate polling recalibration](senate_poll_bias_recalibration.md). The latter replaces the 2018-only directional Senate poll correction and changed conditional Democratic control from the historical 39.5% result shown here to 37.6%. The [prior-winner name-match repair](senate_race_trace_2026.md) brought the current result to 38.1%. The empirical shared-error floor and candidate prior still limit how far polls move a race; other-party candidate-share intervals need separate repair.

## Generic ballot evidence

The [FiveThirtyEight historical generic-ballot trendline](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/master/congress-generic-ballot/README.md) adds 11 as-of election cycles from 1996 through 2016 to research, beyond the one well-populated cycle in our original local archive. At 43 days before Election Day, those reconstructed averages exceeded the eventual Democratic House vote margin by 3.3 points on average, with 3.9-point RMSE. This is in `artifacts/generic_history_research.json`. The series was reconstructed later and may differ from today's NYT poll mix and likely-voter treatment, so its historical bias has **not** been applied mechanically to the 2026 forecast. The combined national-signal covariance remains provisional.
