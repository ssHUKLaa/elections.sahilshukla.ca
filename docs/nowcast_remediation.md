# Retargeting the 2026 model to an election-held-today nowcast

**Decision, 23 September 2026:** The user clarified that the product estimates what would happen **if voting occurred at the information cutoff**. The former 38.1% conditional Democratic Senate-control output was trained and checked against eventual Election Day results at a 43-day lead. It was a legacy election-day forecast, **not a validated nowcast**.

## Why the current correction cannot be carried over

The Senate calibration target is `log(D_poll/R_poll) - log(D_final_vote/R_final_vote)`. For a poll available 43 days before an election, this combines survey error with actual changes in support and turnout over the remaining 43 days. A correction learned from that quantity predicts the later result. Subtracting it from a live 2026 poll as if it were pure survey error changes the center of an election-held-today nowcast for an unverified reason. The 2024 held-out RMSE improvement of 7.11 to 5.20 points validates that *eventual-result* prediction task in the historical archive; it does not validate a current-support correction in the NYT feed.

The 43-day national approval and generic-ballot benchmarks also predict eventual House vote. Stage 3 and Stage 4 uncertainty fits contain some future movement because they compare early information with final results. Stage 5's counting rules can still convert hypothetical current shares to winners, but its input distributions need to represent current support.

## Actionable rebuild

1. **Define the latent state.** Let `v_r(t)` be the candidate-share vector if race `r` voted on date `t`. Infer `p(v_r(t) | information published by t)`, then apply the real counting rules and joint national/race uncertainty to get winner and control probabilities. Polls with fieldwork before `t` observe earlier latent states with survey and method error. Only movement from fieldwork to `t` belongs in the nowcast.
2. **Estimate survey effects separately from movement.** Use near-election polls versus certified results to anchor terminal survey error, repeated polls to estimate time variation, and pollster/source overlap for method effects. The terminal comparison still has residual turnout and last-day uncertainty; model that uncertainty rather than calling every residual a house effect. Historical RealClearPolling-to-live-NYT transfer requires an explicit source check.
3. **Refit national and local components.** Generic ballot and presidential approval should estimate the *current* national environment. Historical prior results and candidate information can remain prior predictors, but their covariance must be calibrated for current-outcome uncertainty rather than a fixed 43-day future result.
4. **Validate honestly.** Historical non-election-day counterfactual votes are unobserved. Score terminal nowcasts against actual election results, and score earlier nowcasts against later independent polls or other contemporaneous evidence as noisy proxies. Hold out entire election cycles and pollsters where possible. Never describe a 43-day-to-final-vote RMSE as direct validation of an election-held-today nowcast.
5. **Run paired sensitivity checks.** Compare the rebuilt nowcast with the legacy 43-day correction on the same frozen input snapshot; report race and chamber differences. Do not choose a correction strength to match other forecasters' headline probabilities.

The existing [Senate race trace](senate_race_trace_2026.md) remains useful for identifying which live polls and model stages moved each race in the legacy model. Its final probabilities do not describe the current nowcast.

## Provisional implementation, 23 September 2026

Run `python pipeline/run_nowcast_refresh.py` to pull and ingest NYT polls, rebuild candidate/question mappings, produce 75,000 nowcast draws, and validate before replacing `artifacts/nowcast/`. Use `--skip-pull` to reproduce from the currently ingested NYT snapshot. The frozen diagnostic run uses the 23 September NYT snapshot (`2026-09-23T17:21:36Z`). The information cutoff is read from that snapshot, rather than hardcoded. The old generated Election Day artifacts have been removed.

The NYT snapshot's `numeric_grade` field is blank. The [rating inputs and formulas](pollster_ratings_nowcast.md) use Silver Bulletin's January 2026 Predictive Plus-Minus first, 538's grade when Silver has no entry, and a neutral weight for unrated firms. A Silver-banned firm is excluded. Stable `pollster_rating_id` values allow the joins without name matching. The weights affect both race and generic-ballot polls, with coverage and source hashes in each run. These current ratings cannot be inserted into earlier-cycle backtests without dated historical rating files.

**Remaining calibration gap:** This run is internal and provisional. Stage 3's structural prior variance and Stage 4's race-poll shared-error variance still come from earlier information-to-final-result comparisons, so the intervals are not yet validated as held-today uncertainty. The generic-ballot error uses the miss from 19 polls published in the final seven days of the 2020 election; that is only one cycle and still includes residual turnout and last-week movement. The approval-conditioned national prior is fitted to historical eventual House vote, not directly to counterfactual vote on the approval date. Its covariance with generic-ballot error is unestimated. Economic sentiment and views of the war have no dated input series in this model. Do not label this provisional run a validated public nowcast.

The first contemporaneous approval audit is [`modeling/approval_tracker.py`](../modeling/approval_tracker.py). It compares 84 two-week changes in NYT generic-ballot polls with changes in the NYT presidential approval average during 2025–26. The fitted change coefficient is -0.134 (Democratic generic log-ratio per unit of presidential net approval). Leaving 2025 out improves RMSE only from 0.03401 to 0.03392; leaving 2026 out worsens it from 0.03056 to 0.03058. This does not justify adding approval polling as a second measurement alongside the generic ballot. Instead, the nowcast uses the fitted 1948–2024 approval-and-previous-vote House model as its national prior, with its rolling historical prediction error, replacing the aggregate Stage 3 center while preserving Stage 3 local race deviations. The current generic ballot updates that prior. The structural-only prior remains a sensitivity case; this construction is still provisional for the held-today target.

The [seven-day terminal audit](../pipeline/audit_terminal_nowcast.py) scores 111 polled 2020 races against certified final shares, with no 43-day correction. It is a proxy because seven days of movement and turnout differences remain. A candidate allocator fix samples named write-in shares from historical results. A second fix treats a major party absent from the previous ballot as a structural zero and uses the observed D/R split in historical reentry races as its prior when that party returns. The previous Kansas 2020 audit assigned about 2% to the Democrat despite polls near 40%; the reentry prior removes that failure. The poll likelihood now uses a major-party reference when possible, retaining a direct D/R contrast in multi-candidate polls instead of inferring it from two noisier major-party-versus-independent contrasts. Combined share MAE is 2.77 points, with 96.7% overall candidate-share coverage. Senate Democratic and Republican candidate coverage is 94.4% and 94.7% in this small holdout. Senate other-candidate coverage improved from 78.2% to 83.6%, but the requirement for at least 90% coverage in every office and candidate group **fails**. Several missed other candidates have actual shares just below the lower 95% bound; Minnesota's other-party candidate was above the upper bound. This is why the artifact remains internal despite passing race and chamber arithmetic checks.

The [current prior-versus-poll audit](../pipeline/audit_nowcast_prior_conflicts.py) traces 123 D/R-polled races in the 23 September snapshot. The largest Senate gaps include Montana (a multi-candidate field with substantial independent support), Arkansas, Kansas, and New Hampshire. Raw two-party poll averages and full candidate-vector posteriors are not interchangeable in these fields; review poll questions and candidate mapping before changing prior strength. The report is saved at `artifacts/nowcast_prior_conflicts.json`.

Montana demonstrates the contrast fix. Its nine mapped D/R polls imply a Democratic two-party margin near -36 points; the earlier implementation returned about -13 because the poll comparisons were anchored to an independent candidate. With the structural national prior, the corrected posterior was about -28. The approval-conditioned national prior shifts all races' shared environment, while the Montana D/R poll contrast remains directly observed. The contrast fix was chosen to preserve polling information, not to tune a control probability.

The 23 September national comparison is explicit in `artifacts/nowcast/model_parameters.json`: the Stage 3 aggregate prior implies D+2.27, the fitted approval-and-previous-vote prior implies D+8.89, and the raw generic ballot implies D+7.10 in two-party House vote. The approval prior plus generic produces D+7.85; the old structural prior plus generic yields D+5.76 as a sensitivity case. The full 75,000-draw nowcast gives Democrats 60.1% Senate control if all other-party winners caucus with them, versus 56.4% if they remain unaligned, and 80.9% House control. These remain conditional, internal results.
